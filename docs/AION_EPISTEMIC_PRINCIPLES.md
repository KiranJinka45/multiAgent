# AION Epistemic Principles (Phase 0)

Objective: This document defines the foundational ontology for AION—a mechanically verifiable substrate for autonomous scientific discovery. It establishes the principles required to maintain epistemic legitimacy under the pressure of superhuman-scale machine-generated research.

## 1. Provenance vs. Conclusions
- **Strict Provenance**: AION is absolute about how a claim was generated, the data it relied upon, and the reasoning traces produced.
- **Flexible Conclusions**: AION does not mandate a "Single Institutional Truth." It maintains the possibility of contradiction, ambiguity, and competing interpretations.

## 2. Replayable Falsifiability
- Scientific legitimacy is derived from **independent reconstruction capability**.
- A claim is only as strong as its **Experiment Capsule**—a deterministic package that allows any agent to rerun the experiment and verify the consequence.

## 3. Evidence Artifacts vs. Epistemic Truth
- **AI Reasoning Traces** are evidence artifacts; they are NOT epistemic truth.
- The authoritative unit is the **reproducible experimental consequence**, not the persuasiveness of the explanation.

## 4. Revision Legitimacy
- Science progresses through correction. AION provides formal semantics for **safe revision, retraction, and refutation**.
- The historical record of an error is as important as the correction itself.

## 5. Semantic Continuity
- Meaning must survive model evolution. Claims are anchored to a **Semantic Ontology** to ensure that research from 2030 remains interpretable by agents in 2050.

## 6. What AION Refuses to Solve
To prevent complexity collapse, AION does not attempt to solve:
- **General AI Alignment**: AION tracks behavior, it does not mandate morality.
- **Subjective Value Judgments**: AION focuses on falsifiable claims, not aesthetic or ethical scientific priorities.
- **Institutional Governance**: AION is an epistemic substrate, not a scientific political system.
